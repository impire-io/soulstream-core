# What is rollup?

A busy workbench collects **sticky notes** — every turn, comment, and clipped file is
one more note on the pile. The notebook never forgets, which is wonderful, until a
newcomer has to read five hundred notes just to see where things stand.

**Rollup** is tidying day: someone takes the current first page, lays every note since
on top of it in order, and **glues the result into a brand-new first page**. Same
words, same drawings, same clipped files — one page. The pile of notes is gone; the
page *is* the pile, condensed. The workbench looks exactly as it did; it just reads in
one glance.

## Nothing changes for anyone

Read the topic after a rollup and you see the identical conversation — every turn,
every comment still pointing at the line it commented on, every attachment. New notes
stick on exactly where they would have: the fresh first page remembers which notes
were on top (the *frontier*), so the conversation continues over the seam without a
wrinkle.

## Two people reach for the glue

No one is "the tidier" — anyone may compact, any time. If two people try at once, or
someone posts a note in the very instant you're gluing: **the first one wins, and the
other attempt simply evaporates**. Nothing tears, nothing is lost — the loser just
shrugs and maybe tries again later. There's no rota, no referee, no lock on the glue
pot; the notebook's own rules make the race safe.

And nobody *has* to tidy: an untidied topic works forever, its pile is just taller.

## What the seal means after tidying

The old notes carried their authors' wax seals ([signing](./signing.md)). Gluing makes
one new page — and the old seals go with the old notes. The **tidier presses their own
seal on the new first page**: from then on, that seal vouches for the whole condensed
page. That's the honest trade of compaction — you swap many small testimonies for one
person's attestation of the whole. Realms that treasure the originals archive them
before tidying (a later extension's job).

## When the glued page won't fit

Some workbenches hold big things — a page can only take so much glue. When the
condensed page would be too thick (past the notebook's 128 KB rule), the tidier puts
the whole condensed page **in the supply cupboard** instead, and glues a small **claim
ticket** into the notebook: *"contents in box such-and-such, and here's a fingerprint
of exactly what's inside."*

Readers barely notice — the library fetches the box, checks the fingerprint, and shows
the same page. If the box ever goes missing or its contents don't match the
fingerprint, the topic says so plainly ("I can't read my own first page, here's why")
rather than showing you half a page and hoping.

The careful order makes crashes boring: box first, ticket second. If the tidier
trips before gluing the ticket, the notebook is untouched and there's just one
unclaimed box in the cupboard — clutter, never damage.

## When it happens

- **By hand** — `soulstream rollup <path>`: "save a version".
- **On closing** — closing a topic tidies it too, so it rests as one neat page.
- **On archiving** — the final, thorough tidy. See [lifecycle](./lifecycle.md).

Never by itself. Tidying throws the notes away, so it only happens when someone
deliberately says so — that's a choice with no way back, and Soulstream doesn't make
those on your behalf.
